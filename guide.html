How to use Wird

A walk through the app, screen by screen. Wird has three tabs at the bottom: Today for your daily wird, Mushaf to read and listen freely, and Tafsir. Settings live behind the ⋯ button on the Today screen.

1. Today

Everything starts here. The top card shows today’s wird: the surah, the page, and the three steps ahead.

  • Tap Begin to start. If you stop partway, it says Continue · page N and picks up where you left off.
  • Below: your streak, your pearl jar, your journey, and a look back at the month.
  • At the top: appearance (light or dark), your pearl balance, and ⋯ for settings.

2. Warm up

Five words from today’s page, one at a time. Read the word, then tap Show meaning to see its meaning, its transliteration and its root.

The words are chosen from what you’re about to read, so you meet them again on the page a minute later.

3. Read

The page appears exactly as printed in the Madinah mushaf.

  • Tap any word for its meaning, root and how often it appears in the Quran. From the card you can also bookmark the ayah or add a private note. Swipe down to close it.
  • Listen plays the page; each word lights up as it is recited. Change the speed from 0.75× to 2×.
  • Prefer meanings line by line? Switch to study mode, which lists each word with its meaning, with A− / A+ for text size.

4. Check

A few short questions on the words you met, such as what a word means, or completing the ayah from a word bank.

A wrong answer costs nothing. There are no hearts and no lockouts; the word simply comes back for review sooner.

5. Done for today

Your streak grows by a day. Then your pearls drop into the jar, and if the jar fills, a new stop opens on your journey.

Every seventh day brings a small celebration and 50 bonus pearls.

6. Your streak

Tap the streak card on Today to see your calendar: every day you read, with the Hijri date beneath each Gregorian one.

  • Freezes cover a missed day automatically. You can hold two; buy more with 250 pearls.
  • After a missed day, Wird may offer a few extra pages to catch up. It’s never required.

7. The journey

Each khatm is a journey. The first follows the Seerah: every half juz you read opens the next stop, with the ayat connected to it and a short story.

Every five stops close a chapter with a du’a from the Quran.

8. The Mushaf tab

The whole Quran, to read freely. Swipe to turn pages, like a printed mushaf.

  • Listen keeps going page after page, even with the screen locked.
  • The list button opens Go to: jump by surah, juz or page, open your saved bookmarks, or search by surah, Arabic word or English meaning.
  • The download button opens Offline audio: save any juz, or the whole Quran, for listening without internet.

On iPad in landscape, the Mushaf shows two pages side by side, like an open book.

9. The Tafsir tab

The explanation of each ayah on the page: Al-Mukhtasar in English, al-Muyassar in Arabic, or both together.

10. Roots

From any word card, open its root to see every form it takes and every ayah it appears in. Your progress is counted in roots: about 1,700 in the whole Quran, a number you can actually finish.

11. Settings (⋯)

  • Daily reading: where you read from, pages per day (1–20), warm-up and questions on or off.
  • Reminders: up to five times a day, and today’s wird on the Lock Screen.
  • Reciter, language (English or Arabic) and appearance.
  • iCloud sync between your iPhone and iPad, and family circles.
  • Tasbīʿ, for a khatm every week in the seven traditional parts.

Changing your pace or starting page never resets your streak.

12. On iPad

Everything works the same on iPad, with a few differences:

  • Landscape: the Mushaf tab shows two pages side by side, the odd page on the right like print. Swipe to turn a spread at a time.
  • Portrait: one page in the shape of the printed mushaf, with larger type.
  • The tabs sit at the top of the screen, and Split View works.
  • Your reading syncs with your iPhone through iCloud.
